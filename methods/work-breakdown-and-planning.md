# Work Breakdown and Planning

## Primary source
PMI Practice Standard for Work Breakdown Structures:
https://www.pmi.org/standards/work-breakdown-structures-third-edition

## VERIFIED FACT
PMI describes WBS as a way to organize total project scope and as a foundation/input for schedule, budget, risk and performance tracking. Its guidance applies across predictive, iterative, incremental and agile lifecycles.

## Framework decomposition
A useful engineering hierarchy may be:
`Outcome → Capability → Epic/Feature → Work Package → Macro Task → Micro Task`

The exact hierarchy is adaptive; the important properties are:
- scope completeness;
- no uncontrolled overlap;
- traceability upward to value/requirement;
- dependencies visible;
- completion objectively assessable.

## Forecasting boundary
WBS decomposes scope. It does **not** by itself justify a completion date.

Forecasting additionally needs:
- remaining work;
- dependency/critical-path information;
- actual capacity/availability;
- historical throughput/cycle time where applicable;
- blockers/risks;
- uncertainty.

Maintain distinct fields for:
TARGET DATE, COMMITMENT DATE, FORECAST DATE/RANGE and ACTUAL DATE.
